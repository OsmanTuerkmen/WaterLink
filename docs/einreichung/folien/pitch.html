WaterLink

An active problem, documented by UNICEF

This is not history. UNICEF reports that up to one in three hand pumps does not work at any one time across Africa, and the WHO/UNICEF report of 25 August 2025 counts 2.1 billion people without safely managed drinking water. In Kenya’s public data the newest survey is from November 2024, so nobody can see today which pump works.

The last confirmed state before the walk, and a repair that counts

  1. A public board.Each water point shows its state, who confirmed it and how long that holds. After 12 hours it reads “unclear”, never an old green.
  2. A repair that counts.A report becomes a job with a named person and visible blockers. “Repaired” is not “working”: only a test on site turns it green.
  3. A reliable alternative.If a point is down, the board names the nearest one, but only if it was freshly confirmed and its route was checked.

Live and working end to end: board and household view (English, Kiswahili draft), report → job → blockers → repair → test, history, route and access checks, SMS simulation, a replay of real 2014 pump data, practice mode without login. 85 unit and 37 browser tests run on every push. Places come from real surveys (WPdx); people, confirmations and routes are simulated and labelled so.

What we learned, and what comes next

  1. An old green is worse than no answer.Every confirmation expires.
  2. Repaired is not working.Only a test on site turns a point green.
  3. “Working” is not “safe”.No water-quality claim, said on every board.
  4. A dot on the map is not a recommendation.An alternative needs a checked route.
  5. Records are not places.60 survey records turned out to be 42 places.
  6. Say what is unchecked.The Kiswahili is a labelled draft; no impact is claimed.

Next

One neighbourhood, one partner (a county water office, NGO or maintenance service) and four numbers: fresh confirmations, time to a repair that counts, wasted walks, and whether anyone reads “Working” as “clean water”. Baseline first, targets afterwards.
