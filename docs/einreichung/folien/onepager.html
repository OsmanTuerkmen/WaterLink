WaterLink

Does our water point work today?

WaterLink shows the last confirmed state of every water point before people walk, and makes every outage a job with a named person that only counts as repaired once the pump was tested on site.

The problem is real and current

  • Up to one in three hand pumps does not function at any one time across Africa; people can be without clean water for at least 2–3 days (UNICEF Office of Innovation, “Smart Pumps”).
  • 2.1 billion people still lack safely managed drinking water; 1 in 4 worldwide (WHO/UNICEF JMP report, 25 Aug 2025).
  • The public data cannot say what works today: of 39,959 Kenyan survey records the newest is from 30 Nov 2024, the median 5.7 years old (WPdx, our calculation).

The prototype: working today, not a mock-up

  • Public board and household view, English and Kiswahili (draft), printable.
  • Report → job → blockers → repair → test on site, with history.
  • Stale, conflicting or offline states never look working.
  • Alternatives only with a checked route; add a water point by tap on the map.
  • SMS simulation, replay of real 2014 pump data, practice mode without login.
  • 85 unit and 37 browser tests on every push.

Numbers behind the assumptions

  • Hand pump failures: up to 1 in 3 (UNICEF).
  • Kenya, last survey: 21% non-functional, 27% in Busia (WPdx).
  • Kyuso, Kenya, top complaints: repair cost 19%, repair time 17%, breakdowns 17%.
  • Downtime with sensors and a repair service: 27 days to under 3 (WASREB 2014).
  • Mobile phones: 48.6% of rural, 64.6% of urban people (Housing Survey 2023/24).
  • Open, no evidence yet: will operators confirm often enough, will anyone pay. No pilot, no impact claim.
